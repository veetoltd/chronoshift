Devlog #1 · Week 1 · 16 – 20 Sep 2026

Building a Time-Travel Dinosaur Game in Unreal Engine 5

40 changes

One empty project. One week. A one-kilometre dinosaur world, seven species, a stampeding Brontosaurus herd — and a hero. This is how Chronoshift began.

Cold open

Eight weeks ago, this game did not exist. No dinosaurs. No snow. No hero. Just an empty project and a very big idea.

This is the story of how it's being built — one week at a time.

What is Chronoshift?

Chronoshift is a third-person, time-travelling adventure. You play a lone explorer who is pulled through time by a mysterious brass gauntlet — and every jump drops him into a different era of history.

It starts in the age of the dinosaurs. Jungle, swamp, tar pits, lava fields — and creatures that very much want to eat you.

From there, the journey runs right through history — the Ice Age, the Bronze Age, Ancient Egypt, Rome, the Maya, the Vikings, the Middle Ages, the Industrial Revolution, both World Wars, the modern day — and on into the future. Each era has its own world, its own weapons, its own monsters, and its own boss.

Your tools change with the times. Flint spears and bone clubs. Stone hammers and atlatls. Survival is about reading the land, not just swinging a weapon — stealth in the long grass, fire against the cold, and knowing when to run.

Who's making it

Chronoshift is being made by the Console and PC Gaming channel, together with ITYELABS, the game development studio. Both are part of Veeto Ltd.

If you're new here — Console and PC Gaming is a gaming channel. And this series is something a little different: a behind-the-scenes look at building a game, week by week, warts and all.

You can find the channel at youtube.com/@ConsolePCGaming, and the studio's home on the web at veetoltd.github.io.

How it's being built

The game runs on Unreal Engine 5. And it's being built in a very modern way: one developer, working alongside a team of AI tools.

Claude does a lot of the heavy lifting inside the editor and the code. Meshy and Tripo turn concept art into 3D models. Blender rigs them. ElevenLabs gives the creatures their roars. And Gemini, Grok and ChatGPT help with concept art, design documents and the website.

Every week, we'll show you what was built, what was planned — and, just as honestly, what got thrown out.

Week 1 — Wednesday: the empty project

Week one began on Wednesday the sixteenth of September, with a single save: the first backup of a project still called "Timeshift".

Then came Friday. And Friday was busy.

Friday: a world in a day

In a single day, the blocky test ground was replaced with a real landscape, and the first zone was planted with dense, procedurally scattered jungle.

Then, zone by zone, the world got dressed. A narrow jungle trail. A misty tar pit. A raptor hunting ground. A cliff face where rocks come down. A river you cross on a jam of mossy logs, and a far bank of scorched ash.

And at the very end: a boss arena ringed with glowing lava, basalt spires — and a portal ring, waiting.

By the evening, the whole thing had outgrown its first map. Era One was rebuilt on a one-kilometre landscape with seventeen zones — meadow, swamp marsh, nesting grounds, canyon pass, ash forest and lava fields.

And the world got its first residents. Seven dinosaur types were generated with Meshy, rigged and animated — and before midnight, they were walking around in the game.

Saturday: the herd

Saturday was all about one place: the Grazing Meadow.

The first version looked a bit too much like an English park, so the oak trees came out and palm-like cycads went in, opening it up into a Jurassic savanna.

Then came the Brontosaurus herd — huge, slow, wandering animals with proper collision. And then, of course, we made them stampede. Step into the trigger and the herd charges — and anything in its path gets trampled. Including you.

The rest of the new zones were filled with enemies, each species picking its own model and its own stats automatically.

Sunday: a hero arrives

On Sunday, the grey test mannequin finally stepped aside, and the real Era One hero took his place — the explorer with the red scarf.

The placeholder cylinder weapons were replaced with real models, and glowing weapon pickups were scattered across the era.

What was planned — and what got cut

The plan for week one also included running water through the meadow. Spoiler: that idea would be built, torn out, built again and torn out again — and you'll see exactly why in episode two.

Outro

Week one: forty changes, a one-kilometre world, seventeen zones, seven species of dinosaur, and a hero.

Next week — weapons, stealth, and a spear that really did not want to sit in his hand.

I'm your narrator — thanks for watching, and we'll see you in week two.

Devlog #2 · Week 2 · 21 – 27 Sep 2026

It Took 10+ Tries to Put a Club in His Hand

119 changes

119 changes in a single week — the busiest week of the whole project. The game gets its name, the hero learns to fight back, and the dinosaurs finally get brains.

Cold open

Last week, Chronoshift went from an empty project to a one-kilometre world full of dinosaurs. This week, the hero had to learn how to fight back.

Week two: a hundred and nineteen changes — the busiest week of the whole project so far.

Monday: a name, and a club that would not sit still

Monday started with a name. At two minutes to nine, the project called Timeshift officially became Chronoshift.

Then came the weapons, and a problem that sounds simple: putting a club in a man's hand. The first attempt hung it at his side, head down. The next held it upright by his shoulder. Then like a hammer. Then by the wrong end. It took more than ten attempts, a rebuilt fist, and freshly measured grip bones before the weapons finally sat inside his closed hand.

The spear got a new head — black, knapped flint, lashed to a plain wooden shaft — and, after a few versions, a proper two-handed underarm thrust. The bone club got its own overhead swing.

Monday: stealth

Monday also brought stealth. Press C and the hero crouches — and while he stays low, enemies only notice him at forty-five percent of their normal range.

The first crouch was a deep squat, with legs fighting the foot-placement system, so it was swapped for a sneaking walk from Mixamo. Suddenly, the long grass became somewhere to hide.

Tuesday: the stream that would not stay

Last episode promised the story of the meadow stream. Here it is.

On Tuesday, all the water came out of Era One. A little over an hour later, the meadow stream was rebuilt to match the reference art. Forty minutes after that, it was removed again. The plan had changed.

Not all the water went, though. The swamp got a flooded pond, with a giant ribcage and a clutch of glowing eggs. And that evening, the volcanic zone got its rivers — of lava.

Wednesday: fire and green

Wednesday was about making places feel alive. The lava fields got steam, burning damage along the rivers, a volcano on the skyline, and pterosaurs circling overhead.

The meadow got six new jungle plants, generated with Meshy — horsetails, ginkgo shrubs and tree ferns among them — and the blue mushrooms were quietly dropped.

Every dinosaur got hit animations, so they react when you strike them. And the boss, Rex Prime, finally got a body, with animations driven by the fight.

Thursday: a step back, and a first trailer

Thursday began with a step back. Wednesday's reshaping of the meadow ground, and its new trees, had gone too far — so both were reverted, and the planting was redone in layers instead.

Then came the very first Chronoshift trailer, cut from game footage, with a narration describing the game.

The hero got power-ups: armour as a real system, shown under the health bar, plus health pickups. And along the way, a few hazards turned out never to have been hooked up at all. They hurt now.

Friday: the enemies get brains

On Friday, the enemies got brains. Instead of standing still until they spotted you, every creature now had something to do — grazing, roaming, patrolling. The pterosaurs stopped hovering in your face and started circling overhead.

And six new species arrived: Spinosaurus, Stegosaurus, Parasaurolophus, Ankylosaurus, Dimorphodon, and the small, feathered Kulindadromeus.

The hero answered with a kick for small enemies, an adrenaline power-up, and a torch — with real flame and flickering light. Fire hazards now keep burning you after you step out of them, and thorn thickets joined the list of things that hurt.

The weekend: small things, big difference

The weekend was for the small things that make a big difference. Campfires beside each torch. A floating health bar over enemies. Dinosaurs that no longer walk through each other.

Throwing stones got an aim mode — hold to aim, release to throw — and the stone itself stopped being a grey prototyping cylinder. Weapons moved onto the keys one, two and three.

And Rex Prime, it turned out, had been standing sixty-five centimetres above the ground the whole time.

What got cut

So, what got cut this week? The meadow stream — twice. The blue mushrooms. The dark monkey-puzzle trees. Ten of the twelve egg nests. A river creature called the River Ambusher, replaced by a Spinosaurus — for now. And a whole day of ground sculpting.

None of it is wasted. It's how you find out what the game should look like.

Outro

Week two: a hundred and nineteen changes, six new species of dinosaur, three weapons, a torch — and a game with a name.

Next week: a bestiary that records every creature you meet, flying dinosaurs that refuse to fly, and the first sounds of the jungle.

Chronoshift is made by Console and PC Gaming and ITYELABS. Thanks for watching — see you in week three.

Devlog #3 · Week 3 · 28 Sep – 4 Oct 2026

Our Flying Dinosaurs Wouldn't Fly (So We Rebuilt Them)

70 changes

The flying dinosaurs wouldn't fly — so they were deleted and rebuilt from scratch. Now eleven of them bank, dive and land to feed over the jungle. Plus: the game finally makes a sound.

Cold open

Week two gave the hero weapons and the dinosaurs some brains. Week three was about knowing what you're up against — and about one very stubborn sunbeam.

Seventy changes, and Wednesday alone accounted for thirty-two of them.

Tuesday: the bestiary

Tuesday brought two new species: the small, parrot-beaked Psittacosaurus, and Caudipteryx, a feathered runner about the size of a turkey.

And with the cast growing fast, the game needed a way to keep track. Press J, and a bestiary opens — every creature in the era, with a slowly turning 3D model. Fourteen turnarounds were rendered for it that same day.

Two egg consumables were added too, hidden through the jungle and the nesting grounds.

Wednesday: chasing the sun

Wednesday started in the Canyon Pass. It got light shafts, a darker grade, and a cobbled floor worth walking down. Then it was too dark, so it was brightened. Then brightened again. Then the white glow at the canyon mouth had to go.

After several attempts, the real culprit turned up: the sun itself was in the wrong place. A few more passes after that, the canyon was warm, golden, and readable.

Wednesday: the jungle finds its voice

Wednesday was also the day the game stopped being silent. Footsteps change with the ground under you. Weapons swish. The torch crackles. And the dinosaurs call, growl and cry out in pain when they're hit.

The River Ambusher — cut last week — came back as a real creature, three and a half metres long. And Archaeoceratops joined the bestiary.

Flyers that would not fly

Then came the flying dinosaurs. Flight animations were authored in Blender, sounds were wired up… and they didn't fly. By Thursday morning, they were removed altogether.

Their replacements were built properly from scratch: Pteranodon and Tupandactylus, each with eleven animations. Eleven of them now soar over Era One — banking, diving, and landing to feed.

The dinosaurs also got real, physically based materials, so skin finally looks like skin.

Friday: the Temporal Log

Friday started with a sling and a bola, given proper models and weapon slots — and within a few hours, both had been removed completely. They didn't feel right yet.

The afternoon went into rebuilding the bestiary as the Chronoshift Temporal Log: a brass-framed journal with gears, clock faces and gauges, clickable tabs, and creatures that only appear once you've discovered them — saved between sessions.

The weekend

Sunday was quiet: the first copy for the trailer and the forums, and wind blowing through the ferns, reeds and cycads. Enjoy that wind while it lasts.

What got cut

Cut this week: the first flying dinosaurs. The sling and the bola — for now. And several versions of a canyon that was lit by a sun in the wrong place.

Outro

Week three: seventy changes, five new species, eleven flyers, a bestiary, and a game you can finally hear.

Next week: a tyrannosaur gets a jaw, a voice and a mouth — and is then deleted.

Chronoshift is made by Console and PC Gaming and ITYELABS. Thanks for watching — see you in week four.

Devlog #4 · Week 4 · 5 – 11 Oct 2026

We Deleted Our T-Rex

54 changes

He got a jaw, a voice, a mouth and a bite. By Thursday afternoon, the T-rex was gone. This is the story of why we deleted our boss — and kept only his brain.

Cold open

Week four was supposed to be the week of the tyrannosaur. He got a jaw, a voice, a mouth and a bite. And by Thursday afternoon, he was gone.

Fifty-four changes. Here's what happened.

Monday: new skins and smarter herds

Monday started with a fresh coat of paint: eleven creature species were retextured, and the tyrannosaur was given muted earth tones.

The herds got smarter too. Prey animals now run when danger comes close — and they warn each other, so one startled animal can send a whole herd moving.

And last week's wind in the foliage? It came out on Monday. It just didn't look right.

Tuesday: wading in

On Tuesday, the hero stopped walking on water. He now wades through the swamp, which was deepened from ankle-deep to wade-deep, with a broken-up, natural shoreline.

The boss got a tell before he strikes, better spacing, and a second attack. And deep inside the enemy logic, one thousand one hundred and thirty-five orphaned nodes were deleted in one go.

The bestiary rows were rebuilt to read at a glance, colour-coded by what each creature eats.

Thursday: the mouth

Thursday was the tyrannosaur's big day. His mouth interior looked too much like a tongue, so it was reshaped, then dropped. His model turned out to be two thousand seven hundred and seventeen loose pieces, so it was welded into one surface. Holes in his throat were closed. He got a voice.

Then the bugs. He restarted his animation every frame while turning. He walked his collision right over the player — so a fix went in to push you clear if he ever swallowed you. His rest pose was open-mouthed. And when that was fixed, his jaw was locked shut, so he couldn't bite at all.

Thursday afternoon: delete

At the end of all that, the decision was made: delete the tyrannosaur's art, and keep only his brain. It was time to start again with a better model.

The brain — the fight logic, the tells, the attacks — was kept, ready for a better body. You'll see that body next week. And the one after it.

Friday: the portal and the diet

Meanwhile, a clearing deep in the jungle was dressed with moss and damp, rust-brown earth. And the portal at the end of the era now needs three key pieces before it will open.

Friday ended with a big tidy-up. The project moved its largest files into Git LFS, and twenty-two oversized environment meshes went on a diet — from about six hundred and seventy megabytes down to forty-two. One jungle treeline alone went from eighty-seven megabytes to under three.

What got cut

Cut this week: the tyrannosaur's body. The wind in the foliage. A mouth that looked like a tongue. And over a thousand pieces of dead logic nobody was using.

Outro

Week four: fifty-four changes, eleven retextured species, one welded tyrannosaur, deleted — and a project that's six hundred megabytes lighter.

Next week: the T-rex comes back — three new versions in a single week.

Chronoshift is made by Console and PC Gaming and ITYELABS. Thanks for watching — see you in week five.

Devlog #5 · Week 5 · 12 – 18 Oct 2026

Three T-Rexes in One Week

52 changes

Last week the T-rex was deleted. This week he came back — in three different bodies. One spawned upside down underground. One got 28 animations. And one became the boss.

Cold open

Last week, the tyrannosaur was deleted. This week, he came back — in three different bodies.

Fifty-two changes, and almost all of them had teeth.

Monday: rex number one

On Monday, a brand-new T-rex was built: new mesh, new rig, six animations. And then the fun began.

He was invisible — because he had spawned upside down and buried underground. He had a hole in his belly. He shuffled. And even after he died, he could still stomp you, and a bite he had queued up could still kill you.

His skin and eyes were matched to a reference photo — measured, not guessed.

Tuesday and Wednesday: twenty-eight clips

By Wednesday, he had twenty-eight animations: alert, lunge, stomp, turns, two proper deaths, and four new attacks — plus twelve new sounds, ending in a long bellowing death roar.

One mystery got solved along the way: killing the rex sent you back to the start. It wasn't the rex at all. It was falling ash.

Then every clip was planted on the ground, and his walk was locked to his feet, so he stopped floating and sliding. And then, on Wednesday, he was taken out of the arena again, to clear it for a rebuild.

Thursday: rex two and rex three

On Thursday, a second rex, also generated with Meshy, was rigged with a first walk cycle — and parked.

A third, generated with Tripo and rigged in Blender with a moving tail and jaw, got seven animations and went straight into the game as the boss, Rex Prime. He follows you with his head, his footsteps land with his feet, and he charges at six metres a second.

Combat got some weight too: a short hit-stop, smooth knockback — and a hurt camera shake that was removed the same day, because it just felt bad.

Friday: the volcano

Friday brought the big set piece: a volcano eruption under a dusk sky, an overhauled boss arena, and resolution options in the settings.

On Saturday, the eruption was removed again, to be redone properly.

Also this week

In between the rexes: seven hundred and forty-six props in one jungle clearing were stood upright, and nearly ninety floating ones were dropped back onto the ground. The lighting got a filmic grade. And the hero can now aim and throw stones.

What got cut

Cut this week: the first rebuilt rex. The second one, parked. A camera shake. And a volcano eruption that will be back.

Outro

Week five: fifty-two changes, three tyrannosaurs, twenty-eight animations for just one of them, and a boss who finally fights back.

Next week: swamp hazards, morphing into a dinosaur, and a raptor pack that hunts together.

Chronoshift is made by Console and PC Gaming and ITYELABS. Thanks for watching — see you in week six.

Devlog #6 · Week 6 · 19 – 25 Oct 2026

Raptor Packs, Swamp Traps & Turning Into a Dinosaur

23 changes

Raptors that hunt as a pack. A power-up that turns you into a dinosaur. Sinking mud, tar pits and steam geysers. This is the week Chronoshift started to feel like a game.

Cold open

Week six had fewer changes than any week before it — twenty-three. But each one was enormous. This is the week the game started to feel like a game.

Monday: into the swamp

Monday started with the basics. The hero now begins empty-handed, carries his weapons on his back, and reaches into a hip pouch to throw a stone.

Then the swamp got dangerous. Wading slows you down, with ripples, spray and sloshing sounds. And four hazards were added: sinking mud, a tar pit, timed steam geysers, and a rockfall trap.

Tuesday and Wednesday: power-ups

On Tuesday, three new power-ups arrived. Fury. Regeneration. And Morph — which turns you into a dinosaur.

The dinosaurs got a variety of attacks, and on Wednesday, five more moves were added — shared by the enemies and by you, when you're morphed.

By Friday, the power-ups had their own looks, the pickups were remade, the portal got its ring, and you could even morph into a flyer.

Saturday: the raptor pack

Saturday was a monster. Raptors now hunt as a pack, working together to bring you down. Two hundred and thirty-six new enemy animations went in.

And the jungle came alive: plants bend as creatures push through them, leaves fall, birds scatter, footprints are left behind.

Sunday: every voice

Sunday gave all fifteen species new voices, generated with ElevenLabs, and a dino inspector for checking every animation and sound.

The Triceratops got a full suite of twenty-four animations and smarter behaviour. The Parasaurolophus got its own set too. Dinosaurs now walk to the water to drink, and the meadow was relaid with a winding trail and open grazing lawns.

And that same week, two new trailers were cut — including the first cinematic one.

What got cut

Very little got cut this week. Just a bad habit: dinosaurs walking on the spot. And a bola that kept snaring the player instead of the enemy.

Outro

Week six: twenty-three changes, four swamp hazards, three power-ups, a raptor pack, two hundred and thirty-six new animations — and two trailers.

Next week: a jungle gorge built from concept art, a vine that eats you, and a new boss for the volcano.

Chronoshift is made by Console and PC Gaming and ITYELABS. Thanks for watching — see you in week seven.

Devlog #7 · Week 7 · 26 Oct – 1 Nov 2026

A Vine That Eats You + A Brand-New Volcano Boss

19 changes

A vine that snares your legs. A giant millipede that ambushes from the undergrowth. And a new end boss with a very hard head. Week seven had one rule: if there's concept art, build it like the concept art.

Cold open

Week seven was about one rule: if there's concept art, build it like the concept art. Nineteen changes — a jungle gorge, a vine that eats you, and a brand-new boss.

Monday: new ground

Monday pushed the world outward. The north hills were dressed, a cliff-and-river valley was cut along the meadow's southern edge, and a strange pattern of diagonal stripes on the ground was finally tracked down and removed.

The hero got a stone-framed HUD with a panel for when you're morphed, and the morphed Pteranodon learned to fly properly — without its wings crossing over in a dive.

Tuesday: the makeover that didn't make it

Tuesday morning, a coastal ridge went in south of the first path, with a stream, a rocky coastline and sea crags. Then the canyon got a full makeover with photo-scanned sandstone and desert plants.

It didn't look like Chronoshift. Within hours, the whole makeover was reverted — and the canyon was rebuilt instead as a deep jungle gorge, matched shot for shot to the concept art.

Tuesday: things that want to eat you

The gorge came with company. The Constrictor Vine lies in wait and snares your legs — and by Wednesday it had a braided, mossy look, a firefly glow, a knot you can strike, and a pink, clawed maw.

Arthropleura — a giant, armoured millipede — ambushes from the undergrowth. And four more jungle hazards were added: a living log, a set of tiger jaws, a steam vent, and rubble that slides down slopes.

Wednesday and Thursday: the volcano

Then the end of the era was rebuilt to the concept art. A volcano arena of cracked slabs, spires and lava, under a burning sky. A thin rock bridge over a glowing canyon, with huge footprints pressed into it.

Those footprints belong to the new end boss: Pachycephalosaurus, the dome-headed charger. The fight has a trench, falling lava bombs, a T-rex on the ridge — and an eruption that builds as you go.

Thursday's playtest added an entrance cinematic, a boss health bar and a new volcano.

What got cut

Cut this week: the canyon makeover, gone the same day. And a batch of rocks that had been settled onto the ground — which then had to be un-settled.

Outro

Week seven: nineteen changes, a jungle gorge, a vine with a mouth, a giant millipede, and a new boss with a very hard head.

Next week: the dinosaur era closes its doors — and the Ice Age begins.

Chronoshift is made by Console and PC Gaming and ITYELABS. Thanks for watching — see you in week eight.

Devlog #8 · Week 8 · 2 – 8 Nov 2026

Our Game Just Time-Travelled to the Ice Age

17 changes

Step through the portal and jump over sixty million years. This is the week Chronoshift travelled through time for the first time — from the dinosaur jungle into a frozen Ice Age taiga, with a dire wolf pack waiting.

Cold open

Week eight is the week Chronoshift travelled through time for the first time. Seventeen changes — and one of them was a whole new era.

Monday and Tuesday: finishing the jungle

Monday began with a step back: a more photo-real rebuild of the end level didn't feel right, so it was reverted and the dinosaurs got their original skins back.

Then the jungle's edge was matched to the concept art — a painted volcano on the skyline, a smoke plume, low sun and god rays through the trees.

On Tuesday, the volcano canyon was rebuilt to the concept as well, with a live eruption system.

Thursday: the cave, and the jump

On Thursday, the volcano cave was rebuilt, and a new creature moved in: Pulmonoscorpius, a giant scorpion that crawls across the walls.

And then the big moment. The Dinosaur Era was closed for now, and the portal was opened. Step through, and a cinematic carries the hero through time — into a snowy taiga forest, wearing furs. Welcome to the Ice Age.

Friday and Saturday: wolves and snow

Friday gave the Ice Age its first predators: a dire wolf pack, with sixteen animations, twenty-eight sounds and pack behaviour. By Saturday they could gallop, follow an alpha, fear your torch, and go for your legs.

The snow got boot prints, kicked-up powder and soft ground. A first snowstorm was built. And creatures got physics: flinches, swinging tails, bending saplings and breakable logs.

The weekend: a front door

And the game got a front door: a title screen, with its own music — Cross the Ages — and a switch to turn it off.

Sunday brought a new settings menu, with DLSS, frame generation, Reflex, headphone audio and volume sliders, a new installer, and a five-minute film built entirely from game footage.

What got cut

Cut this week: the photo-real end level. Creature foot placement, switched off because the wolves slid around on folded legs. And, for now, the Dinosaur Era itself — closed while the Ice Age gets built.

Outro

Week eight: seventeen changes, a giant scorpion, a wolf pack, a title screen, a film — and a jump of over sixty million years.

Next week: a frozen lake you can fall through, a giant sloth, and the Ice Age arsenal.

Chronoshift is made by Console and PC Gaming and ITYELABS. Thanks for watching — see you in week nine.

Devlog #9 · Week 9 · 9 – 13 Nov 2026

Fall Through the Ice & Swim to Survive

20 changesLatest

Walk onto thin ice and it cracks, breaks into floating plates — and drops you into freezing water. But you don't just die. You swim under the ice, hunting for a way back up while the cold eats away at you.

Cold open

Week nine brings us right up to date. Twenty changes, all in the Ice Age — and a lot of them about ice.

Monday: a full-size Ice Age

On Monday, the Ice Age grew to full size: a landscape over a kilometre long, later widened with a steppe and a mountain backdrop.

The cold became a real threat. Your warmth drains in the open, and campfires bring it back. And high on a cliff, a falcon's eyrie was built — with a falcon, rigged and ready.

Wednesday: megafauna

Wednesday brought the giants. The short-faced bear, Arctodus, rears up on its hind legs. And Megatherium, the giant ground sloth, was modelled, rigged and given twelve animations, with snow clinging to its fur.

The hero got an atlatl, a spear-thrower. Branches now spring back as you push through them, shedding snow.

The sloth was also meant to dig burrows. They didn't work — so they were removed.

Thursday: the lake

Thursday was all about the frozen lake. Walk onto thin ice and it cracks — in two stages — then breaks into floating plates, with slush spraying up and a freezing mist hanging in the air. By the evening, fifty-eight physics-driven shards were bobbing in the water.

And if you fall in, you don't just die. You swim — under the ice, in a deep basin, looking for a way back up — while the cold eats away at you.

Thursday and Friday: a new hero, new weapons

The hero was rebuilt as a new base body, ready to wear a different outfit in every era. A fur outfit with cloth physics was tried, and swapped for the original hero's face in furs.

And the Ice Age arsenal began: a stone hammer, a flint knife for silent kills, and a flint spear that jabs and counters. Spear throwing is on its way.

Planning has also started on the eras still to come, including Ancient Egypt.

What got cut

Cut this week: the sloth burrows. The cloth-physics outfit. And, as ever, a few dozen versions of things that weren't quite right.

Outro

That's the whole story so far: nine weeks, four hundred and fourteen changes, two eras, and one hero.
